## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

The PSP Gateway experienced prolonged upstream response unavailability. Local request handling remained available, but the external authorization path did not provide usable responses.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 81 minutes, from 2026-02-12T09:00:00Z to 2026-02-12T10:21:00Z. The service recorded 14,700 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

Responders checked the boundary between local acceptance and upstream completion, suppressed unnecessary retries, and reconciled uncertain authorization references after recovery. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation does not classify this event as change-related. Recovery observations, the initiating mechanism, and longer-term remediation are retained as separate parts of the record. A proposed follow-up remains a proposal until its own decision and implementation evidence exists.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Interpretation limits

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Payment enquiries can concern a present interruption, an earlier failure, or a customer-visible outcome. Those questions require different evidence. Service identifiers and timestamps help prevent a recent change note from being mistaken for a status report. A record of symptoms can guide investigation before a cause is established, while a completed postmortem explains the event it actually examined. Operational communication should preserve that difference when handing work between support, engineering, and incident coordination.
