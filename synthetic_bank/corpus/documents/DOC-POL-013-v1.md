# Privileged review prior policy

Fictional Northstar Bank internal material. All organizations, systems, and events are synthetic.

<a id="section-01"></a>

## 01. Governing requirement

Privileged access reviews must identify the account, privileged resource, business justification, and accountable owner. Review decisions and resulting access changes must be retained for later audit examination.

<a id="section-02"></a>

## 02. Supporting detail

This earlier version relies on scheduled owner attestations but does not prescribe the revised monthly workflow. Review completion requires an actual decision record; an invitation to review access is not evidence that the access was examined.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Scope and interpretation

The original audit finding must be assessed against the obligations in force at the time. Later remediation cannot be backdated into the original control record.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Business and service context

Access-control assurance depends on a chain from requirement to implementation to demonstrated operation. A plan shows intended work; a release shows a mechanism was installed; review evidence shows what actually happened during an operating period. Each record has a different author and purpose. An auditor needs the relevant population and disposition history to assess coverage, while the action owner needs unresolved items to remain visible until their completion can be evidenced.

<a id="section-05"></a>

## 05. Application and evidence

When applying this standard, retain the effective version with the decision record. Identify the subject, requested action, applicable conditions, and evidence supporting the disposition. A later revision must not be substituted into an earlier assessment without stating the change in time. Exceptions require an attributable decision through the relevant authority; silence, a missing record, or a successful technical result must not be interpreted as implicit authorization.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Application example

A request to an owner asking whether access is still needed is the beginning of review activity. The completed review requires a recorded disposition that can be connected to the access item. If the owner does not respond, the evidence remains incomplete rather than becoming an approval by default. This example explains the evidence obligation in the earlier standard without importing a later workflow or assuming that a remediation mechanism already existed in the original review period.
