## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

Retries crossed the MerchantConnect payment boundary without a merchant-scoped idempotency key after the July retry redesign. Interrupted acknowledgements could trigger independent execution, and duplicate debits were observed.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 23 minutes, from 2026-07-21T09:00:00Z to 2026-07-21T09:23:00Z. The service recorded 44,700 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

The response contained the affected retry path and reconciled business instructions with settlement references. Investigators separated repeated network attempts from independently executed debits rather than treating the error counter as a duplicate count. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation links the event to preceding change CHG-118. The mechanism above explains the relation; the timing is not the sole basis for attribution. Recovery observations, the initiating mechanism, and longer-term remediation are retained as separate parts of the record. A proposed follow-up remains a proposal until its own decision and implementation evidence exists.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Interpretation limits

The 44,700 failed or rejected attempts are not the number of duplicate debits. The report does not record an exact currency loss or establish that every retry-family event shares this mechanism.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

A payment operation can produce several network attempts. The commercial instruction, the transport attempt, and the settlement entry must remain distinguishable throughout investigation. An interrupted acknowledgement leaves uncertainty about the operation's result; it does not establish that repeating the request is harmless. Interface contracts and persistence boundaries determine what a retry means. Evidence should connect these boundaries through stable references before an analyst classifies an apparent duplicate or recommends further processing.
