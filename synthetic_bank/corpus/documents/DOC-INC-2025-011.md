## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

PayFlow database connection-pool waits grew behind long-running transaction locks. PF_DB_POOL_WAIT identified the database acquisition boundary rather than the outbound PSP client pool.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 24 minutes, from 2025-09-25T09:00:00Z to 2025-09-25T09:24:00Z. The service recorded 7,200 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

Operators identified the blocking transaction path, constrained competing work, and restored database access while retaining request outcomes for reconciliation. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation does not classify this event as change-related. <a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Payment enquiries can concern a present interruption, an earlier failure, or a customer-visible outcome. Those questions require different evidence. Service identifiers and timestamps help prevent a recent change note from being mistaken for a status report. A record of symptoms can guide investigation before a cause is established, while a completed postmortem explains the event it actually examined. Operational communication should preserve that difference when handing work between support, engineering, and incident coordination.
