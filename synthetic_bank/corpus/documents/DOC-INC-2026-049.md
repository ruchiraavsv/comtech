## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

PayFlow checkout workers exhausted their outbound MerchantConnect allocation during a concentrated traffic burst. The constrained resource was the outbound request pool, not PayFlow's database connection pool.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 25 minutes, from 2026-08-01T09:00:00Z to 2026-08-01T09:25:00Z. The service recorded 7,000 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

Responders separated outbound waiting from database acquisition metrics, limited checkout admission, and preserved pending outcomes for status reconciliation. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

<a id="section-05"></a>

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation does not classify this event as change-related. That classification does not imply that the service had never been deployed or modified; it describes the evidence for this particular interruption. Recovery observations, the initiating mechanism, and longer-term remediation are retained as separate parts of the record. A proposed follow-up remains a proposal until its own decision and implementation evidence exists.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Interpretation limits

This is a PayFlow capacity event. It should not be counted as a MerchantConnect-owned capacity incident merely because MerchantConnect was the downstream dependency.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Payment enquiries can concern a present interruption, an earlier failure, or a customer-visible outcome. Those questions require different evidence. Service identifiers and timestamps help prevent a recent change note from being mistaken for a status report. A record of symptoms can guide investigation before a cause is established, while a completed postmortem explains the event it actually examined. Operational communication should preserve that difference when handing work between support, engineering, and incident coordination.
