## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

An external PSP accepted connections but responded slowly enough that MerchantConnect requests exceeded their processing window. The provider degradation persisted after local worker availability was confirmed.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 75 minutes, from 2025-12-16T09:00:00Z to 2025-12-16T10:15:00Z. The service recorded 12,500 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

The incident response reduced repeated calls, retained unresolved operation references, and waited for verified provider recovery before returning to normal throughput. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

<a id="section-05"></a>

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation does not classify this event as change-related. That classification does not imply that the service had never been deployed or modified; it describes the evidence for this particular interruption. Recovery observations, the initiating mechanism, and longer-term remediation are retained as separate parts of the record. A proposed follow-up remains a proposal until its own decision and implementation evidence exists.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Interpretation limits

Although MerchantConnect was affected, the confirmed family is external PSP degradation. Counting every merchant timeout as local capacity exhaustion would misclassify the event.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Merchant payment processing crosses an outbound provider boundary. Waiting work, active connections, and client-visible responses describe different parts of that journey. A useful investigation preserves those distinctions and relates observations to the particular configuration and procedure in force. A temporary reduction in waiting work can be operationally useful without explaining why the original pressure accumulated. Service identity and observation time therefore remain essential when comparing records that use similar words for different failure modes.
