## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

A MerchantConnect deployment reset retry counters when work moved between workers. Operations consequently exceeded the intended retry allowance and received downstream rejections.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 19 minutes, from 2025-12-02T09:00:00Z to 2025-12-02T09:19:00Z. The service recorded 17,100 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

Operators compared retry counters across worker handoff, reverted the affected release, and reconciled outstanding request identifiers before resuming normal retries. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

A payment operation can produce several network attempts. The commercial instruction, the transport attempt, and the settlement entry must remain distinguishable throughout investigation. An interrupted acknowledgement leaves uncertainty about the operation's result; it does not establish that repeating the request is harmless. Interface contracts and persistence boundaries determine what a retry means. Evidence should connect these boundaries through stable references before an analyst classifies an apparent duplicate or recommends further processing.
