## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

A campaign spike reached SettlementHub after a consumer configuration change had reduced effective processing capacity. EventMesh lag delayed settlement consumption and reconciliation.

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 49 minutes, from 2026-05-18T09:00:00Z to 2026-05-18T09:49:00Z. The service recorded 15,300 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

## 04. Response and recovery

The response compared consumer progress against the campaign window, restored the preceding consumer setting, and drained outstanding events while preserving downstream ordering. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Settlement and reporting follow payment acceptance through asynchronous processing stages. Each stage can complete at a different time, and a report can be accurate for its stated cutoff while omitting more recent work. Investigation therefore follows source identifiers through event consumption, reconciliation, and publication. Delay, mismatch, and accounting correction are separate observations. Combining them under a single label without tracing their respective records can conceal both the actual processing bottleneck and the required control response.
