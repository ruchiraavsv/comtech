## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

A PayFlow deployment changed retry classification so terminal declines re-entered the retry queue. Repeatedly attempting a terminal outcome consumed processing capacity and generated further rejections.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 18 minutes, from 2026-08-03T09:00:00Z to 2026-08-03T09:18:00Z. The service recorded 28,000 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

Operators restored the previous classification behavior, removed confirmed terminal outcomes from the retry path, and reconciled ambiguous requests separately. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

<a id="section-05"></a>

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation links the event to preceding change CHG-206. The mechanism above explains the relation; the timing is not the sole basis for attribution. Recovery observations, the initiating mechanism, and longer-term remediation are retained as separate parts of the record. A proposed follow-up remains a proposal until its own decision and implementation evidence exists.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Interpretation limits

This is a retry classification defect, not evidence of missing merchant-scoped keys in MerchantConnect. A terminal decline must not be retried merely to seek a different answer.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

A payment operation can produce several network attempts. The commercial instruction, the transport attempt, and the settlement entry must remain distinguishable throughout investigation. An interrupted acknowledgement leaves uncertainty about the operation's result; it does not establish that repeating the request is harmless. Interface contracts and persistence boundaries determine what a retry means. Evidence should connect these boundaries through stable references before an analyst classifies an apparent duplicate or recommends further processing.
