## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

## 04. Response and recovery

The team compared the effective setting with the release baseline, reverted the configuration change, and checked recovery through the customer-visible status path. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

## 06. Interpretation limits

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Payment enquiries can concern a present interruption, an earlier failure, or a customer-visible outcome. Those questions require different evidence. Service identifiers and timestamps help prevent a recent change note from being mistaken for a status report. A record of symptoms can guide investigation before a cause is established, while a completed postmortem explains the event it actually examined. Operational communication should preserve that difference when handing work between support, engineering, and incident coordination.
