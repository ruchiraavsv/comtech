## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

A MerchantConnect worker-lease deployment prematurely released retry ownership during acknowledgement reconciliation. Multiple workers attempted the same reconciliation task, producing rejected attempts and delayed resolution.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 15 minutes, from 2026-08-27T09:00:00Z to 2026-08-27T09:15:00Z. The service recorded 17,200 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

The team reverted the lease change and restored single ownership of pending reconciliation tasks. Persisted operation records were retained while outstanding outcomes were resolved. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

## 06. Interpretation limits

The reviewed defect concerns reconciliation ownership after the duplicate-prevention release. This report does not record renewed duplicate debits or universal failure of the idempotency fix.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

A payment operation can produce several network attempts. The commercial instruction, the transport attempt, and the settlement entry must remain distinguishable throughout investigation. An interrupted acknowledgement leaves uncertainty about the operation's result; it does not establish that repeating the request is harmless. Interface contracts and persistence boundaries determine what a retry means. Evidence should connect these boundaries through stable references before an analyst classifies an apparent duplicate or recommends further processing.
