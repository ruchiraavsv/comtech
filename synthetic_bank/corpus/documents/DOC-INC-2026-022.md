## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

A SettlementHub database-index deployment increased lock contention during reconciliation writes. The resulting database waits delayed processing of otherwise available settlement work.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 38 minutes, from 2026-03-19T09:00:00Z to 2026-03-19T09:38:00Z. The service recorded 11,800 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

The response compared write latency with the release baseline, reverted the index change, and reconciled outstanding work after locks cleared. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

<a id="section-05"></a>

## 05. Change assessment and follow-up

The investigation links the event to preceding change CHG-209. The mechanism above explains the relation; the timing is not the sole basis for attribution. Recovery observations, the initiating mechanism, and longer-term remediation are retained as separate parts of the record. A proposed follow-up remains a proposal until its own decision and implementation evidence exists.

<a id="section-06"></a>

## 06. Interpretation limits

The event is database contention associated with a change. It must not be relabeled as EventMesh backlog solely because settlement completion was delayed.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

Settlement and reporting follow payment acceptance through asynchronous processing stages. Each stage can complete at a different time, and a report can be accurate for its stated cutoff while omitting more recent work. Investigation therefore follows source identifiers through event consumption, reconciliation, and publication. Delay, mismatch, and accounting correction are separate observations. Combining them under a single label without tracing their respective records can conceal both the actual processing bottleneck and the required control response.
