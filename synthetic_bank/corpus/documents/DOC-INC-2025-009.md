## 02. Observed failure and confirmed mechanism

A PayFlow retry worker lost a lease acknowledgement and scheduled an already-pending status attempt again. Repeated retries consumed the operation's permitted attempt budget.

<a id="section-03"></a>

## 03. Impact and measurement boundary

The attributable interruption lasted 16 minutes, from 2025-09-05T09:00:00Z to 2025-09-05T09:16:00Z. The service recorded 12,800 failed or rejected attempts. This is an attempt count, not unique payments or customers: one business instruction can generate several attempts. Unique customer impact and exact currency loss were not recorded. No assumed transaction value has been attached to the attempt count.

<a id="section-04"></a>

## 04. Response and recovery

The response suspended the affected retry path, reconciled pending status references, and restarted work only after distinguishing original operations from repeated attempts. The handover retained observation times and the original service and operation references. Responders separated uncertain outcomes from confirmed failures so that recovery did not silently convert pending work into successful or lost payments.

## 06. Interpretation limits

The reviewed defect is in retry scheduling. The event does not record executed duplicate debits or evidence that a merchant idempotency boundary was absent.

<a id="section-07"></a>

## 07. Operational context

A payment operation can produce several network attempts. The commercial instruction, the transport attempt, and the settlement entry must remain distinguishable throughout investigation. An interrupted acknowledgement leaves uncertainty about the operation's result; it does not establish that repeating the request is harmless. Interface contracts and persistence boundaries determine what a retry means. Evidence should connect these boundaries through stable references before an analyst classifies an apparent duplicate or recommends further processing.
